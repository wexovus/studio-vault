# Roman history explained under 3 minutes

Sent by Nicolas on 2026-09-26 as a reference for **how to tell a story worth
watching**, after the first stick-figure Tesla cut came back as a fact list.

1080x1920 vertical, 60fps, 2:58, ~9,800 likes. Description: "ROMAN HISTORY
EXPLAINED UNDER 3 MINUTES".

Artefacts: `/mnt/bulk/studio-media/artifacts/ref_ddn4/` (video, 23 frames,
contact sheet). Transcript: [[things-explained-rome-3min-transcript]].

## What it is

A complete history of Rome — founding to the fall of the West to the Byzantine
continuation — in 597 words of continuous narration at ~201 words/min, with no
dead air and no digressions.

## The structure

1. **The hook IS the promise.** First line: "Rome history explained, under
   three minutes." It states the subject, the format and the constraint in nine
   words. Nothing is teased that is not delivered.
2. **Strict chronology, no detours.** Founding → kingdom → republic → Punic
   Wars → Caesar → assassination → Augustus → empire → Christianity → division
   → 476 → and then the correction.
3. **Constant on-screen labels.** Every beat carries its date and its name as
   burned-in text: "753 BCE", "Romulus", "Remus", "Tiber River", "509 BCE",
   "Senate", "Republic", "Punics", "49 BCE", "Rubicon", "March 15th 44 BCE",
   "27 BCE", "Augustus", "395 CE", "476 CE". The labels, not the drawings, are
   how a viewer tracks the story. The video also carries a permanent header
   ("ROME HISTORY") for branding.
4. **The turn near the end:** "But here's an important point. Rome didn't
   simply disappear in 476." It reframes everything before it, so the ending
   pays off the promise rather than merely stopping.
5. **Compression by omission, not summary.** No scene is explained. Each event
   gets one clause and the next one starts.

## The visual grammar

Hybrid: minimalist stick/blob figures with thick black line art composited over
semi-rendered painterly backdrops (architecture, landscape, maps). Period
costume carries role — helmets, togas, crowns, capes. Maps and architectural
diagrams do the explanatory work (a three-tier forum diagram for the
constitution; a single landscape showing a settlement on the left growing into a
city on the right, joined by an arrow). Comedic shorthand for violence.

**Crucially: the faces are NOT recognisable portraits.** The audit of 23 frames
found "abstract white discs" with dot eyes and "deliberately non-specific"
features on every named historical figure. Identity is carried entirely by
costume plus the on-screen name label. Nobody in the niche is drawing likeness.

## Why it was worth filing

It is the clearest specimen we have of the format Nicolas wants for
company-history: **dense, chronological, labelled, promise-first, and short.**
See [[animated-narrative-explainer]], which now carries the comparison against
the calm-essay register.

The face finding cuts against our own plan in a useful way: a recognisable
cartoon portrait is an UPGRADE on what currently works in this niche, not an
imitation of it. We are not copying a convention; we are the only ones doing it.

## Limits

- Follower count and RPM are unknown; 9.8k likes on an unverified account is a
  weak performance signal, so treat the FORMAT as the finding, not the numbers.
- No sources are cited on screen, and it is a rapid summary: several of its
  claims (the Romulus/Remus framing, "traditional date") are the legend, not the
  history. Our own bar is higher — claim-level sourcing — so we take the
  structure and leave the sourcing standard alone.
