## Two winning registers (and their content is not ours)

**A. The suppression hook (3.13M).** Opens "Your doctor will never tell you
this. Billion-dollar supplement companies are hoping you never find out."
Then a cost contrast ("less than 10 cents" vs "no pill on a pharmacy shelf"),
a symptom checklist aimed at the demo ("if you're over 60 and you have even
one of these…"), a time-bound promise ("what happens in the first 15
minutes"), then real-ish biochemistry (polyphenols, chlorogenic acid).
Fast cutting, 2.9s median. **We may not use this**: suppression framing and
cure claims are exactly what our channel `never_show` bans. Borrow the
checklist and the time-bound promise; refuse the conspiracy.

**B. The calm essay (2.58M).** Rhetorical question stack ("Have you ever
wondered why Japan has one of the longest life expectancies… Why their cities
are spotless?") → negation pair (**"It's not genetics. It's not luck. It's
habits."**) → paradox premise ("so small they seem almost insignificant. But
that's exactly why they work") → eight numbered habits, each with cultural
grounding (kaizen, 1% better) → aphoristic close + subscribe ask. Slow
cutting, ~20s median. This is the register [[second-wind-defect-chain-2026-09-25]]
was reaching for and did not reach.

## The load-bearing devices (all six sources)

1. **One protagonist who experiences the thing** — the IG room and the IG
   Louisiana reel both carry a single recurring character (a mustachioed
   stick figure; chibi Napoleon). The viewer follows a person, not a topic.
2. **Two- to four-word punches after long sentences.** "You become the
   sound." / "Your brain is making it." / "They saw it anyway." The IG reel
   averages a punch every ~40 seconds and every one lands on a beat change.
3. **Every abstraction is carried by a named study**: 1953 Heller & Bergman,
   80 adults, 94%; 2009 Mason & Brady at UCL, 200+ screened → 19 → 9/10;
   2004 Harvard, 13 people, 96 hours, 10 hallucinated; 1951 Canada's Defence
   Research Board, Donald Hebb, McGill, **$20 a day**. Person + institution +
   date + n + result. Our fact ledger already has exactly this shape.
4. **An escalation ladder** — three or more progressively extreme studies, so
   the video keeps topping itself without new claims.
5. **A mid-video myth debunked** ("the 45-minute claim came from a British
   newspaper in 2012… Orfield says the quote was made up"). Buys credibility
   cheaply and creates a turn.
6. **Return to the opening image, then an aphorism.** "So go back to the room
   in Minneapolis… The room does not make you hallucinate. It just stops
   covering for the fact that you always were."
7. **Text on screen as beat labels, not subtitles** — "0 dB", "HELLO?",
   "GRRRWL", "MADE UP", "NOT REAL", "DAY 5", "CONFESSION", "$20/DAY",
   "CONTROLLED HALLUCINATION". They mark story position.
8. **A hook that states the ending's shape without spoiling it** — the IG
   Louisiana reel: "offer to buy just one city… ended up buying the entire
   territory for almost the same price… let's start from the beginning."


## Third register: the dense chronological explainer (added 2026-09-26)

Reference: [[things-explained-rome-3min]] — "Rome history explained, under three
minutes". Sent by Nicolas as the model for how to tell a story worth watching,
after our first stick-figure cut came back as a fact list with no reason to keep
watching.

It is a different shape from A and B above, and it is the one that fits a
history channel:

| | calm essay (B) | dense explainer (C) |
|---|---|---|
| rate | ~145 wpm, unhurried | **~201 wpm, continuous** |
| length | 16 min | under 3 min |
| structure | question → negation → numbered list → aphorism | strict chronology → turn → close |
| hook | rhetorical question stack | **the promise itself**: "X explained, under three minutes" |
| viewer's anchor | the recurring character | **on-screen labels** — dates, names, places, constantly |
| ending | aphoristic close | a **turn** that reframes what came before |

### The five rules worth copying

1. **State the promise in the first sentence.** Subject + format + constraint.
   Promise nothing you do not deliver.
2. **Chronology is the spine.** One clause per event, then move. Never stop to
   explain a scene — compression by omission, not by summary.
3. **Label everything on screen.** Dates, names, places. In this format the
   burned-in labels are the comprehension device; the drawing is the delivery.
   Our renderer already supports beat labels — they need to be far more frequent
   and factual than "one per story beat".
4. **End on a turn, not a summary.** "But here's an important point…" reframes
   the whole film. A fact list stops; a story lands.
5. **Burned-in header for branding**, present the entire runtime.

### The face finding

**Nobody in this niche draws a recognisable face.** The audit of all 23 frames
found abstract white discs with dot eyes on every named figure; identity comes
from costume plus the name label.

So a portrait head (a cartoon version of the actual person, built from their
photographs) is an **upgrade on the format**, not a copy of it. We would be the
only ones doing it. It also solves the thing labels cannot: a viewer who joins
mid-video still knows who they are looking at.

## Why this matters for the pipeline


Animation is deterministic. That is the whole argument: a vector scene with a
defined character cannot grow a robot, cannot clip a body through a wall, and
cannot swap a 70-year-old for a 30-year-old between shots — the three defects
that survived every AI-generation guardrail we built. The format the niche
rewards is also the format that removes our failure modes.

The studio already ships the machinery: `remotion/` (React → video) and
`hyperframes/`, plus `studio/cards.py` and `studio/diagram.py` for graphics.
